Machine learning for nucleic acids

On designing RNA with models, and coming to biology from physics.

I'm a Canadian machine learning scientist at Lila Sciences, on the nucleic acid team, where I build models that design and optimize RNA. Before that I worked on RNA 3D structure prediction at Atomic AI, on the core models like ATOM-1 as well as the scientific computing tooling around them.

A product of Edmonton, Alberta, I'm currently living in San Francisco. In a previous life I thought I wanted to be a physicist, but I've since realized that I'm broadly interested in working on cool problems with cool people. Over the years I've performed my best impression of "too interdisciplinary to function": computational physics, computer vision, robotics, space science and machine learning, on both the theory and the applied side.

I'm particularly interested in problems at the intersection of machine learning and the natural sciences, especially where there's a chance to make a positive impact, and I'm excited about the flurry of activity in structural biology and materials science right now. I'm drawn to combining the current strengths of learned models with the power of traditional computational methods, for example pairing Boltzmann generators or all-atom structure prediction with docking and free-energy methods.

I received a B.Sc. with Honors in Physics and an M.Sc. in Computing Science, specializing in statistical machine learning, both from the University of Alberta. My thesis, supervised by Csaba Szepesvári, was on pure exploration in multi-armed bandits. I also did a research internship with the Advanced Concepts Team at the European Space Agency, using machine learning to analyze chaotic trajectory data and riding endless Dutch bike paths with childlike glee.
